How we originate a line

We cannot read bookmaker prices off OLBG - they are rendered client-side and were missing from every page we retrieved (see Flagged for review). So the board is priced by us, from the one numeric market signal OLBG does print: the tip split for each selection.

CAL-1, the engine behind 116 lines

StepWhat happens
1. PriorThe market structure sets the starting point: 0.50 for a two-way market, 0.3333 for a three-way money line, 0.125 for an outright (assumed eight runners, which is an assumption and is labelled).
2. ShrinkageThe printed split is pulled toward that prior with 6.0 pseudo-tips: p = (wins + K × prior) / (total + K). This stops a 1/1 tip from becoming a 100% certainty and a 6/6 from becoming a 1.00 shot.
3. Fair price1 / p.
4. Our card price1 / (p × (1 + 0.05)) - a 5% margin, so every price we publish is shorter than our fair number.
5. AmericanStandard conversion: (dec − 1) × 100 at 2.00 and above, −100 / (dec − 1) below 2.00.
6. Confidencehigh needs fair probability ≥ 60%, a sample of at least 8 tips and a price inside 1.4 to 101.0. medium is fair probability ≥ 50%. Everything else is low. The label is computed, never typed.

CAL-2, used for Handball only

OLBG's handball listing prints "Tie" as the most-popular selection for nearly every Champions League match, which makes the printed split unusable (flag F-002). For those five matches we price from the competition base rate OLBG itself publishes - EHF Champions League men, favourites won 446 of 654 games (68.2%) - shrunk 15% toward 50% to allow for what we do not know about the specific tie. The results and standings we cite are official EHF data.

CAL-3, used where there is no market at all

One pick (Antonelli, top-six finish in Mexico City) sits in a market with zero tips. It is priced by us from the championship arithmetic and is labelled CAL-3 so it can never be mistaken for a market-derived number.

The −250 to +10000 band

−250 American is 1.4 decimal; +10000 is 101.0. The band is applied to our card price. A strong favourite priced under 1.40 is a real pick but is excluded from the high-confidence tier, because beating a 1.20 shot is not the part of the market the brief wants us working. Recorded constants: margin 0.05, pseudo-count 6.0, band 1.4-101.0.

Known limits of this approach

  • A tip split is a crowd signal, not a probability. Shrinkage reduces the damage from small samples; it does not manufacture information.
  • Only 116 of 121 inventoried events printed a split. Events with no split are left unpriced rather than guessed.
  • The engine prices the selection OLBG highlights. Other outcomes in the same market stay unpriced unless the event page was read.
  • Confidence describes the strength of our number. It says nothing about whether the bet wins.
