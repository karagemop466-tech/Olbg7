How every line on this board is made

Two inputs, one blend, one margin. The same rule is applied to a EuroLeague game, a Pro D2 match, a greyhound race and a golf tournament, and where an input is missing the line says so rather than guessing.

1. The market input

OLBG prints, for every selection, how many tips it has and how many tips the whole market has. That is the only crowd signal available, and it is thin: the median market on today's card has four tips in it. So the observed share is pulled towards a prior that depends on the shape of the market:

Market shapePriorWhy
Two-way (win match, money line, handicap, totals, puck line, run line)0.50A named side in a two-outcome market
Three-way (handball, rugby union, ice hockey money line)0.42Room for a draw
Outright / tournament0.10A field of many runners

The blend is chance = (tips for the selection + K × prior) / (tips in the market + K), with K = 6. K is the number of pseudo-tips that have to be overcome before the crowd moves a price far from the prior, which is what stops a 2-of-2 split being read as a 100 per cent certainty.

2. The form input

Where an official competition table was read, each side gets a strength figure (win percentage in basketball and handball, points percentage in the NHL, series state in the World Series). The home side gets a 0.05 edge and the difference is put through a logistic with scale 0.7:

chance(home) = 1 / (1 + exp(-((strength home - strength away + 0.05) / 0.7)))

For handicap, puck-line, run-line and spread markets the expected margin is derived from that same chance (margin = sigma × inverse-normal(chance)) and the line is then covered or not against a normal distribution with the sport's own spread: basketball 11.5 points, ice hockey 1.9 goals, baseball 3.2 runs, handball 6.5 goals.

3. The blend, the margin and the band

Where both inputs exist the line is 50 per cent market and 50 per cent form. Where only one exists the other is absent rather than zero. Then price = 1 / chance / 1.02, a 2 per cent margin, converted to American odds. A 2 per cent margin means a 70 per cent chance lands on -250, which is the floor of the band the brief asks for, so anything the table makes a stronger favourite than that is published but labelled outside band.

The band is -250 to +10000. 142 of 159 lines fall inside it; 4 are shorter than the floor.

4. The confidence rule

LabelRuleCount today
Highchance of 64 per cent or better and either eight or more tips in the market or an official table behind it11

Unratedno tips on the market at all, so the line is the bare prior4

The rule is deliberately hard to satisfy. A 70 per cent chance from four tips is not evidence, and a 55 per cent chance from forty tips is not either. High needs both a number and a reason to believe the number.

5. What is not in any line

  • Bookmaker prices. OLBG renders odds client-side and they were absent from every page retrieved, so no line here can be compared with the market. That is the single biggest limitation of this board.
  • Injuries and team news. No feed was readable for any of the fifteen sports.
  • Weather, referee appointments, travel. None was read, so none is priced.
  • Snooker and greyhounds. OLBG published no markets, so those two sports have no lines at all.
