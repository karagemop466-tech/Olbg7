How we originate a line

We cannot read bookmaker prices off OLBG - they are rendered client-side and were missing from every page we retrieved (see Flagged for review). So the board is priced by us, from the one numeric market signal OLBG does print: the tip split for each selection.

CAL-1, the engine behind 142 lines

StepWhat happens
1. PriorThe market structure sets the starting point: 0.50 for a two-way market, 0.3333 for a three-way money line, 0.125 for an outright (assumed eight runners, which is an assumption and is labelled).
2. ShrinkageThe printed split is pulled toward that prior with 6.0 pseudo-tips: p = (wins + K × prior) / (total + K). This stops a 1/1 tip from becoming a 100% certainty and a 6/6 from becoming a 1.00 shot.
3. Fair price1 / p.
4. Our card price1 / (p × (1 + 0.05)) - a 5% margin, so every price we publish is shorter than our fair number.
5. AmericanStandard conversion: (dec − 1) × 100 at 2.00 and above, −100 / (dec − 1) below 2.00.
6. Confidencehigh needs fair probability > 70% (strictly greater; exactly 70% stays medium), a sample of at least 8 tips and a fair decimal price inside 1.4 to 101.0. medium is fair probability ≥ 50% (including 70%+ shots whose price falls outside the band). Everything else is low. The label is computed, never typed.

CAL-2, the fallback for unusable splits (no rows on this board)

OLBG's handball listing prints "Tie" as the most-popular selection for 15 of the 19 rows on today's card, which makes those splits unusable (flag F-002). The CAL-2 fallback exists for exactly this situation: price from an independently sourced competition base rate rather than from the defective split, and label the estimate. On this board no CAL-2 rows are published at all - the 15 "Tie" rows are excluded, and only the four rows with a real club selection are priced through CAL-1.

CAL-3, the fallback for markets with no split (no rows on this board)

CAL-3 covers a market that prints zero tips, where no reprice is possible. It would price from explicit championship or event arithmetic and carry a visible estimate label. Today's board contains no such pick; the one inventory row without a split (T20 Blast 2027 outright) is left unpriced and flagged instead.

The −250 to +10000 band

−250 American is 1.4 decimal; +10000 is 101.0. The confidence gate applies the band to the fair decimal (1 / p_fair), because it describes market odds: a 70% shot has a 1.43 fair price but a 1.36 card once the 5% margin is applied, so testing the margined card would make the 70% rule and the band mutually exclusive. The card price keeps its own separate in-band flag on the data table. A strong favourite under 1.40 remains a real, published pick but is excluded from the high-confidence tier. Recorded constants: margin 0.05, pseudo-count 6.0, band 1.4-101.0.

Known limits of this approach

  • A tip split is a crowd signal, not a probability. Shrinkage reduces the damage from small samples; it does not manufacture information.
  • Only 142 of 143 inventoried events printed a split. Events with no split are left unpriced rather than guessed.
  • The engine prices the selection OLBG highlights. Other outcomes in the same market stay unpriced unless the event page was read.
  • Confidence describes the strength of our number. It says nothing about whether the bet wins.
