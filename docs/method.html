What this site does

We read OLBG's own tip pages for fifteen sports, record every selection that carries tips, and then originate our own price for each selection. A price is turned into a pick, a confidence label and — for every pick — a written prediction of at least thirty words.

OLBG does not publish decimal odds for most of these markets. It publishes how many tipsters backed a selection out of all tips on that market. That share is the only market input we have, so the method below is built around it.

Pricing method

Step 1 — tip share. s = tips / total. A share is not a probability: 1 of 1 is a 100% share and nowhere near a 100% chance.

Step 2 — shrink to the base rate. Each market has a number of possible outcomes n_out, so the base rate is b = 1 / n_out. With n tips on the market and K = 10:

w = n / (n + K)    p = w·s + (1 − w)·b

One tip moves the price by roughly a tenth of the way to the base rate; forty tips move it by four fifths.

Step 3 — price with a margin. We quote a decimal price of 0.975 / p, i.e. a 5% theoretical overround split between the two sides. American odds are derived from that.

Step 4 — odds band. Picks must price between −250 (decimal 1.40) and +10000 (decimal 101). Rows outside the band are still priced and shown, marked “not in band”.

Step 5 — confidence. high needs n >= 8 tips on the market AND p >= 0.62; medium needs n >= 3 AND p >= 0.58; low is everything else. A single tipster on a 100% share can never be high.

Step 6 — one pick per event. The eligible row with the highest modelled probability wins the event, breaking ties on tip count and then on market type (match winner, then handicap, then total). Each sport's card is the five highest-confidence events; where fewer than five exist, the site says so.

Model inputs that are assumptions, not facts

The number of outcomes in a market is an input we set. For two-team markets it is two; for a ninety-minute handball line three; for a Test match three (two sides plus the draw). For outright markets we use the size of the field: 32 for the All-Ireland football, 12 for hurling, 12 for Super League, 17 for the NRL, 2 for State of Origin, 6 for the Six Nations, 96 for the PDC World Championship, 128 for a Players Championship, 21 for the F1 drivers' title, 14 for the cricket World Cup, 8 for the WBBL, and 70 for each golf tournament (the Mexico Open page listed more than eighty golfers; the other two fields were not published, so 70 is an assumption and is flagged). Greyhound races are treated as six-dog races.

What was checked, and what was not

Checked against an official source: NHL standings for every hockey pick (NHL.com, page dated 26 October 2026) and NBA standings for every basketball pick (NBA.com, 2026-27 regular season). Those are the only official form numbers in the output.

Not checked, therefore never claimed: injuries, line-ups, team news, weather, travel, rest days, head-to-head records, player rankings and cricket series context. The ATP rankings page and the ICC team rankings page were both requested and both rendered without a data table, and the European league sites could not be read from here, so those fields stay empty rather than guess. Each prediction names what it could not check.

Tipster comments are opinions. We do not repeat them as fact — one captured comment on the Dallas/Miami game credits Miami with Giannis Antetokounmpo, who plays for Milwaukee. That error is logged on the flagged page.

Every market row links to the OLBG page it came from. Form numbers are only stated where an official league page was read (NHL.com, NBA.com); everything else is attributed to OLBG's published tip counts. Unconfirmed fields are left blank and listed on the Flagged for review page. Our prices are analytical output, not a bookmaker offer.
